About the Associate Program

What we cover

Four kinds of risk work

Our workshops, speakers and interview prep rotate through these four areas.

Forensics & Financial Crime

When a company's numbers don't add up, forensic teams find out why. Trace fraud and money laundering through millions of transactions, find the controls that failed, and build the evidence, in cases as large as Wirecard's €1.9 billion in cash that never existed.

Fits CS, AEM, Economics, Government

Risk Modeling

A spreadsheet error in a JPMorgan risk model understated the risk behind a $6 billion trading loss. Build and test the models banks use to lend and trade, along with the checks meant to catch errors like that one.

Fits CS, ORIE, Statistics, Economics

Litigation Economics

When one company sues another, economists put a dollar figure on the damage. Build the models behind that number from large datasets of sales and prices, in cases as large as Oracle's $8.8 billion claim against Google.

Fits Economics, AEM, CS

Strategy & Analytics

Help a client decide what to do about a risk: whether to enter a market, how to rebuild after a fraud, which processes and controls to redesign. Then back the recommendation with the analysis.

Fits Economics, AEM, Government, CS

Why it's worth a look

A different way into consulting

Every major firm has a risk practice

Risk work sits inside each of the Big 4 and is central to firms like Kroll, FTI and Ankura. The skills you build carry from one firm to the next.

Your major already fits

Analysts come from Computer Science, Economics, AEM, Government and Engineering. What matters is working carefully with data and explaining what you found.

A less crowded path

Most consulting-minded students only apply to strategy roles. Risk practices recruit on their own tracks, and many strong candidates never hear about them.

Firms with risk practices include

FAQ

Questions people ask

Who can join?

Every Cornell student, in any major or year. General membership has no application, and it opens with our first events in spring 2027.

What's the difference between a member and an associate?

General members can come to every event, use every resource and follow the associate training sessions virtually. Associates are admitted by application each semester and get in-person training, one-on-one mock interviews, small-group time with firms and coaching.

Do I need experience in consulting?

No. We start from the basics, and most members will be new to this work.

I'm interested in strategy consulting. Should I still come?

Yes. Risk interviews use the same case and teamwork skills, so you can prepare for both at once.

How much time does it take?

General members come to whatever interests them, and most events run about an hour. Associates commit about 3 hours a week.

Is this an official Cornell organization?

Not yet. We're student-run, in our founding semester, and plan to register with Cornell's Campus Activities office.
